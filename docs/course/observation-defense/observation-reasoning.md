# Observation Reasoning Model

## Verified current public framing

The current Marzano Evaluation Center FTEM overview describes a five-step observation process intended to move the observer from teacher activity toward student evidence.

At a high level, the public process asks the observer to:

1. identify the element or strategy being used and whether it is being used appropriately,
2. identify how the teacher monitors for the desired effect,
3. determine the percentage of students demonstrating the desired effect,
4. determine whether the teacher adapts when fewer than 91% of students demonstrate the desired effect,
5. assign the final score from student evidence and confirm it during the post-observation conference in collaboration with the teacher.

The public FTEM page also describes pre-observation conferences as a place for teachers and observers to review the standards-based plan before the classroom observation.

## Teaching Practice Lab interpretation

The useful mental model is not:

```text
observer watches teacher
        ↓
observer decides if lesson looked good
```

It is closer to:

```text
instructional intent
        ↓
selected strategy
        ↓
desired student effect
        ↓
monitoring technique
        ↓
student evidence
        ↓
judgment about extent
        ↓
adaptation when needed
        ↓
new evidence
        ↓
reflection / feedback
```

This makes observation a causal reasoning problem rather than a checklist scan.

## Pre-observation preparation

Current MEC coaching material supports clarifying:

- standards,
- instructional intentions,
- prior evidence,
- upcoming lesson plans,
- what success will look like,
- specific evidence indicators.

Teaching Practice Lab should therefore train a teacher to answer before an observation:

- What are students expected to know or do?
- What cognitive demand does the target require?
- Why is this strategy appropriate for that target?
- What should students demonstrate if the strategy works?
- What evidence will be visible or collectable?
- What misconception or failure pattern are you watching for?
- What will you change if the desired effect is not present?

## During-observation reasoning

The observer should distinguish three different categories:

### Teacher move

What the teacher says, models, prompts, organizes, or changes.

### Student response

What students say, write, produce, explain, compare, revise, practice, or solve.

### Evidence claim

The conclusion that a desired effect is or is not occurring.

A teacher move is not automatically proof of a student effect.

Example:

```text
Teacher asks students to compare two algorithms.
```

This is evidence that the teacher created a comparison opportunity.

It is not yet evidence that students actually understand the important similarities and differences.

## Adaptation

Current FTEM public framing explicitly asks whether the teacher makes adaptations when the desired effect is not sufficiently widespread.

Teaching Practice Lab should train adaptation as:

```text
weak / incomplete evidence
        ↓
diagnose likely barrier
        ↓
change something instructionally relevant
        ↓
monitor again
        ↓
compare new evidence
```

A change without renewed evidence is incomplete reasoning.

## Defense standard

A defensible explanation should be able to connect:

```text
standard / target
→ instructional decision
→ intended student effect
→ evidence
→ interpretation
→ adaptation
→ next evidence
```

The teacher does not need to sound like a rubric. The teacher needs to make the instructional logic visible.
