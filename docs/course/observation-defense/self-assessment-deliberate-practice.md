# Self-Assessment and Deliberate Practice

Current MEC deliberate-practice material presents teacher growth as focused, intentional improvement rather than trying to improve every element at once.

The public material supports:

- teacher self-assessment,
- selecting one of the 23 high-leverage strategies as a growth focus,
- using observation/self-assessment data to identify the focus,
- gathering feedback,
- reflecting on implementation,
- repeating practice over time,
- reviewing growth with an observer or coach.

## Teaching Practice Lab translation

For each competency, the teacher should be able to run a simple deliberate-practice cycle:

```text
select one focus
      ↓
define what better practice would look like
      ↓
plan one lesson where the competency matters
      ↓
identify student evidence in advance
      ↓
teach
      ↓
collect evidence
      ↓
reflect
      ↓
choose one adjustment
      ↓
try again
```

## Self-assessment questions

Before selecting a focus:

- Which competency do I understand least clearly?
- Which competency do I use but struggle to explain?
- Where do I frequently lack student evidence?
- Which practice do I tend to identify from activity instead of desired effect?
- Which competency is most relevant to a current instructional problem?

After teaching:

- What did I intend students to do cognitively?
- What did they actually do?
- Which evidence surprised me?
- Which students were not represented in my evidence?
- What did I adapt?
- Did the adaptation improve the evidence?
- What should I try next?

## Growth evidence

Growth should be described with concrete evidence rather than generic statements.

Weak:

> I got better at questioning.

Stronger:

> Earlier in the unit, most of my questions asked students to recall definitions. In the latest lesson, I planned three prompts requiring students to explain why a scheduling algorithm produced a specific result, and more students were able to justify their reasoning before I supplied the explanation.

## Recognition of progress

Current MEC material recommends multiple opportunities to demonstrate growth, reflection conferences after observations, mid-year conversations, and recognition of specific progress.

Teaching Practice Lab does not need a badge system to preserve this idea. Progress can be recognized through:

- before/after reflection examples,
- increasingly ambiguous scenarios,
- stronger defense responses,
- better evidence selection,
- cleaner distinctions among nearby competencies,
- classroom examples showing more purposeful adaptation.

## Field-practice template

```text
FOCUS COMPETENCY:

Instructional problem:

What I will try:

What students should demonstrate if it works:

Evidence I will watch for:

What happened:

What I could conclude:

What I could not conclude:

Adaptation made:

Evidence after adaptation:

Next move:
```

No student names or sensitive records are required.
