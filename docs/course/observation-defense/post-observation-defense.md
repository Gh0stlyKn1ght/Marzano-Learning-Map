# Post-Observation Defense

Current MEC material describes the post-observation phase as collaborative rather than merely a score reveal. The public FTEM overview states that final scoring is confirmed in the post-observation conference with the teacher, while current MEC instructional-coaching guidance describes post-visit conversations as evidence-based reflection leading to concrete next steps.

## Teaching Practice Lab goal

Train the teacher to explain instructional decisions with evidence instead of relying on vague statements such as:

> "I thought the lesson went well."

A stronger defense is structured:

```text
I intended...
I chose...
I expected students to...
I monitored by...
The evidence showed...
I noticed...
I changed...
After the change...
Next time...
```

## Defense sequence

### 1. State the instructional intent

What was the standard, target, or learning problem?

### 2. Explain the strategy choice

Why was this instructional move appropriate for the intended cognitive work?

### 3. Identify the desired student effect

What should students have demonstrated if the strategy worked?

### 4. Present evidence

What did students actually say, write, create, explain, practice, compare, revise, or solve?

### 5. Bound the claim

What do you know from the evidence, and what do you **not** know?

### 6. Diagnose the gap

Where did students struggle, and what is your best evidence-based explanation?

### 7. Explain adaptation

What did you change in response?

### 8. Re-check evidence

What happened after the adaptation?

### 9. Name the next instructional move

What would you repeat, remove, change, or investigate next time?

## Current coaching support

Current MEC instructional-coaching guidance recommends post-visit questions that focus on:

- what is working,
- which instructional decisions influenced student learning,
- where students struggled and why,
- what the teacher would change,
- what support or resources are needed,
- how the teacher will know students respond differently next time.

Teaching Practice Lab should use original questions that train the same reasoning rather than copying official scripts.

## Weak vs defensible answers

### Weak

> I put them in groups because group work helps engagement.

### Better

> I grouped students because the task required them to compare two network designs and explain tradeoffs. I was looking for each student to articulate at least one technical distinction and challenge a peer's reasoning. Two groups were mostly dividing the work instead of discussing it, so I stopped the task and required each member to explain one design decision before continuing.

Why this is stronger:

- instructional mechanism is explicit,
- student evidence is identified,
- false positive is recognized,
- adaptation is described,
- the teacher can explain why the activity mattered.

## Evidence-first language

Prefer:

- "The evidence I had was..."
- "I could conclude..."
- "I could not yet conclude..."
- "The students who responded demonstrated..."
- "I changed the task because..."
- "After the change, I looked for..."

Avoid unsupported absolutes such as:

- "Everyone understood."
- "They were all engaged."
- "The strategy worked."

unless the evidence genuinely supports the claim.
