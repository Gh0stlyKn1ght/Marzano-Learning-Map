# Observation and Defense Research

This section prepares the reasoning layer that sits on top of the 23 FTEM competency dossiers.

It is not observer certification, an official Marzano protocol, or a substitute for district evaluation training. It is a Teaching Practice Lab research package for understanding how current FTEM observation, feedback, reflection, and growth are publicly described.

## Current public-source baseline

Current Marzano Evaluation Center material publicly describes a five-step observation process built around:

1. identifying the instructional element or strategy in use,
2. identifying how the teacher monitors for the desired effect,
3. determining how many students demonstrate the desired effect,
4. examining adaptation when the desired effect is not sufficiently widespread,
5. assigning the final judgment from student evidence and confirming it collaboratively in the post-observation conference.

Current MEC coaching and deliberate-practice material also supports:

- pre-visit clarification of standards, instructional intent, and evidence indicators,
- targeted classroom evidence collection,
- post-visit reflection and next-step planning,
- teacher self-assessment,
- focused deliberate practice on selected elements,
- multiple opportunities to demonstrate growth,
- observer calibration and inter-rater agreement as implementation requirements.

## Teaching Practice Lab translation

```text
Before the lesson
What is the standard / learning target?
What are you trying to accomplish?
What strategy or competency is relevant?
What student effect should occur?
What evidence will you look for?

During the lesson
What are students actually doing?
What evidence exists?
How widespread is the evidence?
What is missing?
Did the teacher adapt?
What happened after the adaptation?

After the lesson
What worked?
What evidence supports that conclusion?
Where did students struggle?
Why?
What would you change?
What evidence would you seek next?
Can you defend the decision?
```

The goal is not to memorize evaluation language. The goal is to make instructional reasoning explicit enough that the framework mapping becomes defensible.
