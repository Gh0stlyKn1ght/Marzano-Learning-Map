# Communicating High Expectations for Each Student to Close the Achievement Gap

## Status

**Current element name and domain placement verified. Public detail is sufficient for a cautious baseline, but protocol-depth verification remains open.**

## Verified public-source claims

Current Marzano Evaluation Center material places this element in the **Classroom Environment** portion of Conditions for Learning. MEC describes the classroom-environment elements as supporting student confidence, academic growth, and community, and states that when students know their teacher believes in their potential, motivation and persistence often increase.

Source: `MEC-ELEMENTS`.

## Teaching Practice Lab interpretation

High expectations should not mean identical treatment, harder work for its own sake, or motivational slogans.

The useful interpretation is:

> communicate belief that each learner can meet meaningful academic expectations, while providing the access, support, feedback, time, and opportunities needed to pursue them.

The phrase **close the achievement gap** requires careful handling. Teaching Practice Lab should not invent causal claims or reduce inequity to teacher attitude alone.

## Strong evidence

Original examples:

- Students who need additional scaffolding still work toward the same meaningful conceptual target.
- The teacher avoids assigning permanently lower-level cognitive work based on prior performance.
- Feedback communicates that improvement is expected and attainable.
- Students receive multiple opportunities to demonstrate learning after support and revision.
- The teacher notices patterns in who receives challenging questions, wait time, support, and opportunities to explain.

## False positives

These alone are not sufficient:

- saying “I believe in you,”
- giving everyone the exact same task regardless of access needs,
- assigning excessive workload,
- refusing scaffolds in the name of rigor,
- praising high performers while rarely probing the thinking of struggling students,
- assuming lower prior performance means lower future capability.

## Closest neighbors

### High Expectations vs Relationships

Relationships create trust, respect, and belonging. High expectations concern belief in capability and access to meaningful challenge. Warm relationships without academic challenge can still communicate low expectations.

### High Expectations vs Planning to Close Gaps

The current Planning domain also contains a progress/gap-closing planning element. Planning concerns designing measures and supports in advance. This Conditions element concerns what students experience in the classroom environment and how expectations are communicated and enacted.

## Computer science example

A student enters a networking unit with weak prerequisite knowledge. The teacher does not remove the student from the core reasoning task. Instead, the teacher provides a subnetting scaffold, models one example, checks understanding, and still expects the student to explain the final network design independently.

## Adaptation questions

- Who receives the most cognitively demanding questions in my room?
- Which students receive support versus lowered expectations?
- Am I using prior performance as information or as a ceiling?
- Do students experience challenge as attainable with effort, strategy, feedback, and support?
- Are there patterns in who gets revision opportunities or teacher attention?

## Defense prompts

- How do you distinguish high expectations from simply making work harder?
- What does equitable support look like without lowering the target?
- How do you know your expectations are communicated to every student?
- What evidence would make you question whether your classroom is unintentionally signaling lower expectations to some learners?
