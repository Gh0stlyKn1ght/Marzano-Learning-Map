# Standards-Based Planning Element 3: Planning with Data to Close Gaps

## Status

**Function confirmed by current MEC public material. Exact current canonical title not yet confirmed.**

Current MEC material says Element 3 ensures planning includes measures that track all students' progress and helps close achievement gaps. Older Focused Teacher Evaluation materials commonly use the title **Planning to Close the Achievement Gap Using Data**. Teaching Practice Lab treats that wording as a working/historical label until a current authoritative protocol/report confirms the exact title.

## Verified public-source claims

Current MEC material supports these points:

- planning should include measures of student progress,
- progress should be tracked for all students,
- data should inform planning intended to reduce achievement gaps,
- the planning domain is concerned with purposeful use of evidence before and across instruction.

## Teaching Practice Lab interpretation

This element is not "look at data" and it is not "give more support to students who are behind." The planning problem is:

> What do I already know about learner progress, what evidence will I collect next, and what instructional response will I plan if students are not progressing toward the target?

Useful planning connects data to a decision.

```text
existing evidence
      ↓
identified need / gap
      ↓
planned task or support
      ↓
planned measure of progress
      ↓
next instructional decision
```

## Original examples

### Programming

Prior work shows several students can write loops but cannot explain termination conditions. The next lesson intentionally includes trace-table evidence and a short explanation task before independent coding.

### Computer systems

A diagnostic shows students can name CPU components but cannot reason about bottlenecks. The teacher plans a scenario-based task and a checkpoint that requires students to justify the bottleneck from evidence.

### Networking

Student work shows subnet calculation errors come from binary conversion, not the subnetting concept itself. The next plan includes a brief targeted support step and a measure that separates binary-fluency errors from subnet-design reasoning.

## False positives

These do not establish strong data-informed planning:

- looking at a gradebook,
- sorting students into "high/medium/low" groups with no instructional consequence,
- reteaching the entire lesson to everyone,
- giving easier work to students who struggled,
- collecting data that does not influence the next plan,
- assuming an achievement gap explains why a particular student struggled,
- treating equal treatment as the only definition of fairness.

## Nearest concepts

### Planning with Data vs Using Formative Assessment to Track Progress

Planning with data is a pre-instruction/across-instruction design competency: existing and anticipated evidence shapes the plan. Formative Assessment to Track Progress is a Conditions for Learning competency focused on progress evidence and student ownership during learning.

### Planning with Data vs High Expectations

Planning may use evidence to anticipate support and access. High Expectations concerns the classroom condition in which each student is expected and supported to succeed. The two can reinforce each other but are not interchangeable.

### Planning with Data vs Feedback

Data may identify a need for feedback, but feedback is a response to learners. This planning element concerns how evidence informs instructional design before the next move occurs.

## Adaptation questions

- What evidence tells me this is the actual learning gap?
- Am I planning from a pattern or from an assumption?
- What progress measure will distinguish whether the planned response worked?
- Does my support preserve the intended cognitive demand?
- What will I do if the next evidence shows the gap persists?
- Are there students whose current success masks a different unmet need?

## Defense prompts

- What data changed your lesson plan?
- What would you have planned differently without that evidence?
- How will you know whether the planned support closes the specific gap?
- How are you avoiding lowering expectations while responding to different needs?
- What evidence will trigger your next instructional decision?
