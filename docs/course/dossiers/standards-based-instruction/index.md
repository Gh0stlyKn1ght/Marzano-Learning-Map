# Standards-Based Instruction Research Dossiers

These dossiers prepare the source-grounded knowledge needed to author the Standards-Based Instruction course. They are **research records, not official Marzano protocols**.

## Source status

The Marzano Evaluation Center currently identifies ten Standards-Based Instruction elements and organizes them by cognitive demand. `MEC-ELEMENTS` is the primary public source for the current names and grouping. `MEC-FTEM-CURRENT` establishes the larger FTEM theory of action: strategy, monitoring, student evidence, adaptation, and defensible judgment.

## Dossier rule

Every dossier separates four things:

1. **Verified public-source claims** — statements supported by current Marzano Evaluation Center material.
2. **Teaching Practice Lab interpretation** — our explanation of the instructional mechanism.
3. **Original examples and exercises** — created for this project, not copied from Marzano protocols.
4. **Unresolved items** — details that require an authoritative protocol or additional source before we encode them as canonical.

Do not silently promote an interpretation into a Marzano claim.

## Cognitive-demand map

Current MEC material places the elements in this broad progression:

```text
Every lesson
└── Identifying Critical Content

Building foundational knowledge
├── Previewing New Content
├── Processing New Content
└── Using Questions to Help Students Elaborate on Content

Practicing and deepening
├── Reviewing Content
├── Helping Students Practice Skills, Strategies, and Processes
└── Helping Students Examine Similarities and Differences

Extending thinking / analysis
├── Helping Students Examine Their Reasoning
└── Helping Students Revise Their Knowledge

Knowledge utilization
└── Helping Students Engage in Cognitively Complex Tasks
```

The sequence is useful for course organization, but teachers may use multiple elements within a lesson. The presence of an activity does not itself prove that the intended student effect occurred.

## Shared analysis questions

Every competency should eventually support a teacher answering:

- What learning problem is this element designed to address?
- What should students be doing cognitively?
- What evidence would show the intended effect rather than mere compliance?
- What common classroom behavior can look similar but miss the purpose?
- What nearby element could be confused with this one?
- What would trigger an adaptation?
- What would I do next, and why?
- How would I explain the decision to an administrator or instructional coach?
