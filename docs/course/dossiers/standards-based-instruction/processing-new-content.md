# Processing New Content

**Domain:** Standards-Based Instruction  
**Current status:** Publicly verified element name and cognitive-demand placement  
**Primary sources:** `MEC-ELEMENTS`, `MEC-FTEM-CURRENT`

## Verified public-source claims

Current MEC material places **Processing New Content** in the *building foundational knowledge* group of Standards-Based Instruction. MEC's broader description of this domain emphasizes students doing the intellectual work and teachers selecting strategies that match the cognitive demand of the target.

The public source does **not** provide enough protocol-level detail for this repository to claim an exact current focus statement or desired-effect statement. Those remain unresolved until verified from an authoritative protocol source.

## Teaching Practice Lab interpretation

Processing is about what students do **with new information while they are first making sense of it**. New content should not remain a one-way transmission from teacher to student. Learners need opportunities to organize, explain, represent, question, summarize, connect, or otherwise transform the information into something cognitively usable.

The central question is: **What are students doing that demonstrates they are making meaning from the new content?**

## Strong evidence

Original examples of useful evidence include students:

- restating a new concept accurately in their own words;
- organizing information into a diagram, model, sequence, or representation;
- explaining how parts of a new system relate to one another;
- answering checks that reveal understanding rather than simple copying;
- identifying where their understanding breaks down;
- using a newly introduced idea in an immediate guided example.

## False positives

These may look active without demonstrating meaningful processing:

- copying notes verbatim;
- highlighting everything on a page;
- watching a demonstration without producing a response;
- repeating the teacher's exact wording;
- completing a worksheet that can be answered by locating text without interpreting it.

## Common confusion

### Processing New Content vs Elaborating on Content

**Processing** focuses on making initial sense of new information.  
**Elaborating** pushes students beyond the initial representation by making inferences, explaining relationships, supporting ideas, or extending meaning through questioning.

A strong lesson may move rapidly from processing into elaboration.

### Processing New Content vs Reviewing Content

Processing happens as content is being learned. Reviewing revisits content after initial learning.

## Computer science example

**Topic:** CPU process scheduling

After introducing FCFS scheduling, students do not simply copy the algorithm. They receive a short queue of processes, manually trace execution order, annotate waiting time, and explain why the schedule behaves as it does.

The evidence is in the trace and explanation. The worksheet format itself is not the evidence.

## Adaptation questions

If students can repeat terms but cannot explain them:

- Is the information coming too quickly?
- Do students need smaller chunks?
- Would a visual, worked example, analogy, or model reduce unnecessary load?
- Can students explain the new idea to a partner without reading notes?
- What misconception is visible in their representation?
- What should I change before adding more content?

## Defense prompts

- What were students required to do with the new information?
- How did you distinguish processing from passive note-taking?
- What evidence told you students were ready to move forward?
- What did you change when their representations showed misunderstanding?
