# Open Course Exercise System

Teaching Practice Lab uses exercises to strengthen recall, discrimination, application, and instructional reasoning. Exercises are open, repeatable, and do not require an account, score history, XP, or rank.

## Exercise families

### Recall

Retrieve a concept without notes.

### Recognition

Identify the most relevant practice in a classroom situation and explain why.

### Compare

Distinguish easily confused ideas, such as Previewing New Content versus Reviewing Content.

### Non-example

Decide whether a visible classroom behavior actually demonstrates the intended instructional practice.

### Find the Error

Critique flawed instructional reasoning.

### Evidence Analysis

Choose which observations actually support the claimed desired effect and which are merely surface activity.

### Lesson Design

Given a topic, target, context, and time limit, build an instructional sequence and explain the choices.

### Reverse Identification

Start with classroom evidence and infer which practice or combination of practices may be present.

### Ambiguous Scenario

Some scenarios intentionally do not provide enough evidence. `Not enough evidence` must be a legitimate answer when supported by the scenario.

### Defense Prompt

Practice answering questions a principal, coach, or framework expert might ask. These can be completed aloud, in writing, or with a future optional AI coach.

## Difficulty bands

**Beginner** exercises provide clear context, limited distractors, and explanatory feedback.

**Practitioner** exercises remove direct hints and require comparison among plausible alternatives.

**Expert** exercises may contain ambiguity, incomplete evidence, overlapping practices, and requests for adaptation or defense.

Difficulty is descriptive only. It does not unlock content or contribute to a profile.

## Feedback rule

Feedback should explain the reasoning, not simply reveal an answer.

Weak feedback:

> Incorrect. The answer is B.

Better feedback:

> The activity shows students working together, but that alone does not establish the intended cognitive interaction with content. Look for evidence of what students were required to process, explain, compare, or revise.

## Open-course rule

Every core exercise must remain usable without:

- login,
- learner profile,
- saved progress,
- leaderboard,
- XP,
- rank,
- subscription,
- student information.

A learner may repeat any exercise as often as useful.

## Interactive media

Three.js, diagrams, animation, and interactive components may be used when they improve understanding. They are optional representations of course material, not gates to accessing it.
