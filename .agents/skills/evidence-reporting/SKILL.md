# Evidence and Reporting Skill

## Purpose

Use this skill when designing practice records, reflection workflows, observation preparation, growth summaries, or printable reports.

## Evidence chain

Every reportable record should preserve this chain when applicable:

`intent → strategy → student response → evidence → adaptation → result → reflection → next action`

## Evidence rules

- Prefer direct classroom evidence over vague judgments.
- Prefer aggregate student evidence over identifiable records.
- Distinguish what was planned from what actually happened.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation.
- Do not fabricate missing evidence for a polished report.
- Allow `unknown`, `not collected`, or `not applicable` states.

## Report rules

Reports may:

- summarize records,
- group evidence by domain/practice,
- show growth over time,
- identify recurring reflection themes,
- list planned next actions.

Reports must not:

- claim to be an official district evaluation,
- invent an official Marzano score,
- imply evaluator certification,
- expose student PII,
- convert weak/no evidence into confident conclusions.

## Print-first implementation

Start with semantic HTML and print CSS. Keep report content accessible as text and useful without PDF generation infrastructure.

## Future AI boundary

AI may help organize evidence, ask reflective questions, detect missing fields, or draft a summary from supplied records. It must not create classroom evidence that was never documented.
